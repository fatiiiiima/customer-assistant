Voice agent: how many callers at once?

Load test results and capacity forecast for 100 to 200 callers. 3 November 2026.

In short
  • Measured: on one laptop, with synthetic callers talking to the real voice server at the same time, the wait from the end of a sentence to the first sound stays at about 1 second for 1 to 8 callers (median 0.9 to 1.1 s, 90th percentile under 2 s), with no failed calls. At 12 callers the slow turns get slower; with 16 callers all speaking together it degrades (median 2.4 to 2.9 s).
  • Why 16 fails: the laptop runs out of CPU, and two shared services on it (the voice and the speech recogniser) each handle one request at a time. It is not a fault in the call-handling code: each call costs about the processor budget that the deployment plan assumes (3 to 4 cores for 16 calls).
  • Forecast: a simulation calibrated on these runs predicts that 100 callers hold a median answer of 0.6 s (90th percentile 1.1 s) with 8 voice servers and 5 recognisers, and 200 callers the same with 16 voice servers and 9 recognisers.
  • Not yet done: a real 100 to 200 caller test. It needs rented machines (about 18 processor cores of call handling per 100 callers, plus the voice and recogniser servers and separate machines to generate callers).

1. What was measured, and what it does not show

Synthetic callers (three computer voices, five different conversations: searches, a comparison, a booking) speak through the real server the way the website does, with an open microphone. Each caller times, on its own side, how long after its last word the first sound comes back. The server runs the live settings: speech recogniser Qwen3-ASR, the model Nemotron on Amazon Bedrock, voice Pocket-TTS. Everything ran on one Apple M3 Pro laptop (12 cores) in Dubai, talking to Bedrock in the US.

Limits: the callers' audio is clean (real callers are noisier, so hearing will be worse), 16 is the highest level tried, and the laptop also hosts the speech and voice servers.

At 16 callers the voice server could not keep up (its wait grows with every caller queued behind another), so 69% of sentences fell back to a second, built-in voice, and the recogniser's queue made hearing take 0.9 s. In the deployment design these are shared services that are run as several copies; on this laptop there is one copy of each.

A call-handling worker carries up to 16 calls and needs 3 to 4 processor cores; the autoscaler asks for more workers than 100 or 200 divided by 16 because it lowers each worker's limit as soon as answers pass 1 second.

What the forecast says
  • It is a cliff, not a slope: a server near 90% busy turns a 0.6 s answer into many seconds. Sizing keeps every shared service near 60%.
  • 100 callers: about 14 workers (roughly 50 processor cores), 8 voice servers (about 16 cores) and 5 recognisers (or 2 faster ones without car-name hints). 200 callers: about 28 workers, 16 voice servers and 9 recognisers (or 3).
  • The model is not the limit: it kept the same 0.5 s to first text up to 48 requests at once.
  • A sudden surge from 200 to 300 callers strains the plan until new workers start (about 45 seconds): 6% of callers would be turned away.

6. What is needed for a real 100 to 200 caller test

  1. Machines: one large Linux machine (or several) for the call-handling workers, machines for the voice and recogniser servers, and separate small machines to generate the callers.
  2. A Linux recogniser server: the two recognisers tested here run only on Apple chips. On cloud machines it would be a GPU server (for example the speech model on vLLM), whose accuracy and speed must be measured on recorded calls first.
  3. The voice servers behind one address: not built yet.
  4. Amazon Bedrock's request quota for this key (no limit was reached up to 60 requests a second).
  5. An approved budget for the rented machines for a day or two.

7. Open points

  • Real callers are noisier than the synthetic ones: expect lower hearing accuracy and more recogniser retries than measured.
  • When a voice server is too slow, a second, built-in voice speaks the sentence. It rescued the wait at 8 callers (25% of sentences) but is a different voice.
  • The model connection drops about 1% of requests at 16 callers over HTTP/2; the call handler retries, but this needs watching at larger scale.
  • The model runs on Amazon Bedrock in the US for development only (callers' words leave the company). The deployment plan uses the company's own GPU servers instead.
