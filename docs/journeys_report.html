Voice agent: 1000 people on the site

A script with several kinds of user at once: browsing, talking to the agent, booking a test drive. 4 November 2026.

In short
  • 1000 simulated people used the site at the same time: 970 browsing, 20 talking to the agent, 10 booking a test drive. No page failed, no call was refused or failed, and 30 calls were open at once at the peak.
  • The wait for an answer stayed under half a second at the median (0.44 s) and 1.2 s at the 90th percentile, with 1.3% of answers over 2 s.
  • A smaller run with the real recogniser and voice (308 people) completed 8 of 9 test-drive bookings end to end, with a median wait of about 1 s.

How the script works

  • Browse: opens the site's pages one after another with a 2 to 8 second pause to read each. A first visit also loads the page's scripts and pictures, later visits only the page.
  • Talk: calls the voice agent with a microphone left open, asks questions, hangs up, browses, then calls again.
  • Book: the same, with a conversation that ends in a test-drive booking. Each call has its own day, time, name and phone number. It counts as booked only when the server confirms the booking.

The numbers of each kind are set on the command line, so the mix can be changed to test other situations.

What this does and does not show

  • Shows: the website, the router, the call-handling workers, the database and the booking flow cope with 1000 people when most only browse, as on a real site.
  • Does not show: 1000 people all talking. Only 30 calls were open at once. Real talking calls on this laptop reach their limit at about 8 to 12 callers, because the voice and recogniser handle one request at a time. The forecast for 1000 simultaneous talkers is in the earlier report: about 80 voice servers, a batching recogniser and 60 or more workers on rented machines.
  • One laptop: everything ran on one machine, including the callers. The website server here is a small local program, so page times say little about a deployed site.
